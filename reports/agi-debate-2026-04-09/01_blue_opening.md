# BLUE TEAM OPENING ARGUMENT: AGI's Net Economic Impact Will Be Positive

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The arrival of AGI represents the most consequential economic transformation since electrification — and the historical record, contemporary data, and structural analysis all point toward net positive outcomes. Not uniformly, not painlessly, but unambiguously net positive in aggregate.

---

## 1. UNEMPLOYMENT — AGI Creates More Jobs Than It Destroys

**Net Numbers**: WEF 2025 projects 170M new jobs created vs. 92M displaced = **net +78M jobs globally by 2030**.

**Every Major Technology Wave Created More Than It Destroyed**:
- Industrial Revolution: real wages rose ~50% (1830–1880) after the adjustment period
- Electrification: created electrical engineering, appliance manufacturing, utility infrastructure, telecommunications
- Computing: created web developers, database administrators, UX designers, cybersecurity analysts — job categories that didn't exist in 1980

**The 9% vs. 47% Problem**: OECD task-based analysis finds only **9% of jobs truly at high risk** — one-fifth of the alarmist 47% figure. Most occupations contain tasks that resist automation even when other tasks are automatable.

**New Job Categories Already Emerging**: AI trainers, prompt engineers, AI auditors, AI safety researchers, data center technicians. The jobs we cannot yet name are, historically, the majority of what gets created.

---

## 2. GDP — UNPRECEDENTED PRODUCTIVITY GAINS, BROADLY SHARED

**Goldman Sachs**: +7% global GDP (~$7 trillion) over 10 years; +1.5pp annual productivity growth.

**Distributional mechanism**: Productivity → lower prices → higher real wages for ALL workers, including those not directly in automated sectors.

**New investment ecosystem**: AI CapEx >$600B in 2026; Nvidia +567% revenue in 2 years; $83.6B AI VC in H1 2025; software developer employment +17.9% (BLS).

**GDP growth funds safety nets**: Richer societies can afford retraining programs, expanded unemployment insurance, UBI experiments.

---

## 3. SOCIAL STABILITY — THIS TRANSITION IS MANAGEABLE

**We are not in 1811 or 1929**: 2008 financial crisis hit 10% unemployment in the U.S. — advanced democracies did not collapse. Automatic stabilizers (SNAP, UI, Medicaid) did not exist during Industrial Revolution.

**Timeline is manageable**: Acemoglu finds only 5% of economy affected near-term. AGI arrival spans 2026–2033 with full integration taking decades — time for policy adaptation.

**UBI evidence is promising**: Stockton SEED — recipients found full-time jobs at double the rate of non-recipients.

**Inequality is a policy choice, not a technology destiny**: U.S. Gini rose from policy choices (tax cuts, trade policy, financialization) — not automation. AI taxes, robot levies, sovereign AI dividends can alter distribution without constraining technology.

## 5. PRE-EMPTING RED TEAM

**"AGI is categorically different"** → Cognitive automation exists on a spectrum; adoption complexity scales with generality; Acemoglu's rigorous analysis still finds only 5% near-term impact.

**"GDP gains flow only to capital"** → Historically inaccurate at decadal scale; 401(k)s and index funds mean productivity gains now flow partially to workers; distributional argument is a policy argument, not structural.

**"Social unrest inevitable at current Gini"** → U.S. Gini reached post-war highs in 2019 without collapse; response was expanded safety net (CARES Act); causal arrow matters — inequality rose from policy choices, not automation.

---

## PROBABILITY ASSESSMENT

**78% probability of net positive outcomes** — global real GDP higher than counterfactual, net employment positive within 15 years, democratic institutional stability maintained in advanced economies.

Remaining 22%: transition faster than historical adoption curve, catastrophic policy coordination failure, or AI capability concentration in few actors. These are manageable risks, not arguments against the technology.

**The net is positive. The work is in the distribution.**
